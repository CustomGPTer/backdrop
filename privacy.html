The short version

  • Backdrop sees only the photos you pick. It keeps its own copies on your phone, encrypted, with location, date and camera details removed.
  • Backdrop does not hold Android’s internet permission. It cannot go online, so it cannot send anything anywhere.
  • There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • Copies leave the app only when you make a backup file and choose where to save it.

What stays on your phone

Everything Backdrop keeps is in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read, and every file is encrypted with AES-256 using a key held by Android’s Keystore, so a copy taken off the phone cannot be read.

  • A copy of each photo you add, re-saved so that no hidden details (EXIF data such as GPS location, date and camera model) remain, plus a small preview and the finished home and lock screen images.
  • How each photo is framed, whether it is a favourite, which screens and times it is for, and the order of your sets.
  • Your settings, Safe Mode choices and schedule, and which photo is showing. The app lock PIN itself is not stored: only a salted, one-way hash of it.

Backdrop does not ask for the internet, your location, your contacts, your camera, your microphone or access to your whole photo library.

Your wallpaper and lock screen

A wallpaper is visible to anyone who looks at your phone, and Android shares the wallpaper’s main colours with your launcher so it can match its theme. Choose your photos with that in mind. Safe Mode and home-screen-only photos are there to help.

Backups

A backup is made only when you ask, and goes only where you choose. It holds your sets, your photo copies and their framing, and your settings, but not your app lock PIN. Each part is encrypted with AES-256 using a key made from the password you choose. Without the password it cannot be opened, by anyone. If you save it to a cloud service, that service’s own privacy terms apply to the copy.

The widget and tiles

The home screen widget shows a small copy of the photo on your home screen now, or the neutral wallpaper when Safe Mode is on. The quick settings tiles show no photos.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your photo copies stay until you remove them. Removing a photo, deleting a set or uninstalling Backdrop deletes Backdrop’s copies. Your originals in the gallery are never changed or deleted. Backup files you saved elsewhere are yours to delete.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands.

Security

All files are encrypted with a key that never leaves Android’s Keystore. The app lock PIN is stored only as a PBKDF2 hash, and the lock pauses after five wrong tries. Screenshots of the app are blocked unless you allow them. Android’s own app backup is switched off for Backdrop, so your photo copies are not copied to a Google account without your knowledge. No method is perfect: keep your phone locked and your backup password private.
